## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment: authored, not interchangeable.** Strip the wordmark and this is still unmistakably an IDX tool. Board lots as the entry unit with shares in the core, tick-size steppers that change step by price band, sector names rendered in Indonesian (Keuangan, Perindustrian, Barang Konsumen Primer), IHSG as the benchmark, quote timestamps in WIB, whole-rupiah integers with `id-ID` grouping. The visual world reinforces it rather than decorating it — the indigo appears as full-strength blocks or not at all, figures are monospaced and tabular so columns align, and structure is carried by hairlines instead of shadow. A generic dashboard template would have to be substantially rewritten to become this.

The composition also resists the category default: an asymmetric hero-plus-figures split rather than a row of four equal stat cards, and a flowing definition list rather than boxes.

## Overall Impression

This is a genuinely good interface with one systemic crack and one strategic gap.

The crack: **the app speaks Indonesian everywhere except in its derived ratios.** `-3,93%` sits three lines above `+30.48 pp`. That's not a typo, it's two formatting systems — `Intl.NumberFormat('id-ID')` for money and percentages, raw `toFixed()` for everything computed in the component. For a product whose entire claim to specificity is that it was built to IDX conventions, this is the detail that undercuts it.

The gap: **the app explains its methodology but not its metrics.** Captions like "Computed nightly from stored history" and "free cash flow uses the OCF − capex convention" are excellent and rare. But Altman Z'' and Piotroski F-Score are presented as bare numbers to a reviewer who may have no idea what a good score is.

Biggest single opportunity: **close the loop after a trade.** Recording a transaction is the app's primary action and its highest-stakes moment, and it currently ends with a modal disappearing.

## What's Working

**The error architecture.** Every panel distinguishes "failed" from "empty", names what broke, and offers retry. `Insufficient cash: Rp 4.250.000 short. Deposit more from the portfolio page, or reduce the order.` names the problem, the amount, and two ways out. That's a 4-quality error message and most products never get there.

**The constraint-as-affordance pattern.** The lots slider is bounded by available cash on a buy and by held lots on a sell, so the control itself expresses the rule instead of waiting to reject you. The sell dropdown lists only what you actually hold. This is error prevention done as design rather than as validation.

**The first-run sequence.** Two numbered steps where the order is genuinely enforced by the ledger, with the blocking step performed inline. It doesn't teach the product, it just removes the dead end.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The locale breaks on every derived ratio**
· **Why it matters**: `+30.48 pp`, `1.25×`, `7 / 9`, beta `0.87`, PEG, current and quick ratios all render with period decimals while every adjacent number uses commas. An Indonesian reader sees a foreign number format; a reviewer sees an inconsistency. It appears in **8 call sites** across `SummaryCards.tsx:135`, `Stock.tsx` (368, 493, 515, 566, 570) and `FinancialsPanel.tsx:68`.
· **Fix**: Add a `fmtRatio` / `fmtDecimal` helper to `lib/format.ts` using `toLocaleString('id-ID')` and route every user-facing `toFixed` through it. (`Portfolios.tsx:288` is SVG path data and must stay `toFixed`.)
· **Command**: `/impeccable polish`

**[P1] No confirmation after recording a transaction**
· **Why it matters**: The highest-stakes action in the app ends with the modal closing. Nothing says "recorded". The user must infer success from the page changing behind them, and for a repeated task they reopen from scratch each time.
· **Fix**: A brief confirmation — and "Record and add another", which turns a 5-trade session from five modal round-trips into one.
· **Command**: `/impeccable harden`

**[P2] The metrics are unexplained**
· **Why it matters**: The Stock page presents Altman Z'' (emerging), Piotroski F-Score 7/9, beta, and TWR with no indication of what range is good. PRODUCT.md weights daily use first, but it also says reviewers open this cold — and these are precisely the numbers that look impressive only if you know what they mean.
· **Fix**: Inline `?` affordances on the derived-metric group headers with one sentence each. No help centre needed.
· **Command**: `/impeccable clarify`

**[P2] Fundamentals is a wall of ~40 figures**
· **Why it matters**: Seven groups rendered fully expanded, followed by Financials with three more groups and two tables. It fails chunking and progressive disclosure. The instrument-density rule justifies the *table*; it doesn't require every group open at once.
· **Fix**: Keep Valuation and Profitability open; collapse Per share, Income, Balance & cash, Shares, Dividends behind their headings.
· **Command**: `/impeccable distill`

**[P3] Login heading duplicates the active tab**
· **Why it matters**: The `<h2>` reads "Sign in" directly above a tab also reading "Sign in". Trivially confusing and slightly cheapens an otherwise strong first screen.
· **Fix**: Let the tabs carry the mode and give the heading something with a point of view, or drop one.
· **Command**: `/impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

Selected per the reference table for a **data-heavy / analytics** interface: Alex and Sam. Casey added because PRODUCT.md's stated success is a public live demo, which arrives mostly on phones.

**Alex (Impatient Power User)** — records five trades from today. Gets `/` to focus search and arrow-keys in the ticker dropdown, which is more than most apps offer. Then hits the wall: **no "record and add another"**, so it's five full modal cycles. **No bulk entry.** Fee defaults correctly per side but occupies a field slot he never touches. He'd want a CSV paste and there isn't one.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent)** — much better than it was. Full keyboard path through the combobox, focus trapped and restored in modals, `<main>` landmark, correct H1→H2 outline, every control named, scoped table headers, reduced-motion handled per-effect. Remaining flags: **the sortable table communicates sort direction with a caret icon and a colour shift only** — no `aria-sort` on the headers, so a screen reader can't tell which column is sorted or in which direction. And **the concentration warning is a coloured note with no `role="status"`**, so it won't be announced when allocation refreshes.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User)** — the sticky ticker column and the overflow fix landed, and the initial bundle is now 83KB. But **every primary action sits at the top of the screen** — Cash and Add transaction are in a header 700px above the thumb on a scrolled portfolio page. **No state persistence**: half-filled add-transaction data is lost if she switches apps and the tab is evicted.

## Minor Observations

- The concentration flag states a risk (`ASII is 36,77% of this portfolio`) but offers no action — no link to trim the position.
- "New account" (tab) versus "Create an account" (heading) are two labels for one thing.
- `Realized P&L` only appears when non-zero, so the stats row silently changes column count between portfolios.
- The Stock page's back link always says "Portfolios" even when you arrived from a holdings row, so it's a destination label rather than a return path — correct, but it means there's no true "back to where I was".
- Transactions list caps at 15 with "Show more"; the ledger has no filter or date range, which will bite past a few hundred entries.

## Questions to Consider

- If a reviewer only ever sees the demo portfolio, is the *stock page* — not the portfolio page — actually your strongest surface? It's the one with five years of history, 40 fundamentals and derived solvency scores, and it's currently two clicks deep.
- What would this look like if recording a trade were a first-class surface rather than a dialog? You already have the 1200px measure and a live order preview's worth of data.
- The app knows a position is 36.77% of a portfolio and flags it. What would it take to go from *flagging* a risk to *offering the trade that fixes it*?
- Every number here is derived from an immutable ledger. Is there a version of this product where the ledger itself — not the dashboard — is the main screen?
